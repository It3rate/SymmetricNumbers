# Levels of Complexity

## Premise
A level of complexity isn't something that emerges from the levels below, it is its own space that pre-exists whether something explores it or not. You are a collection of cells, but your cells don't have access to you, nor you to them. They don't know your favorite movie, and can't possible know it. The new problems that are inherent in a higher level space are not created by the lower level spaces that it is made from. In fact the problems are not even comprehensible to the lower space, and the reverse can also be true.
Understanding these complexity space escalltions are essential to understanding the world in general. The simplest way to explore them it to observe what becomes possible as you step through increasing numbers of measures, in a mathematical way.

## Measures and Levels
### Point Level
A single measure is not a point on a line, it is just a 'here'. Imagine a whole bunch of single points swimming around, not detecting with each other in any way. The most they can say about this point is 'right here'. They can't even tell they are moving.

### Length Level
As soon as one measure interacts with another at a distance, they get a concept of distance, and we now have a length. There is a start and end (or end and start) which means we can compare the two points. In spite of having a length, they still can't tell how long they are, because they have nothing to compare themselves to. Note that the previous point level can not possibly know about length, it didn't invent it, and it didn't emerge from it. Any two separate things will have the concept of distance because it has always been a property of separation, not points. You can not mix single points into lengths without them joining this level by sensing separation.

### Compare Level
In this level, these swimming segments of two points can now connect together. This creates a 3 point line (one shared connection point). In 2D we could imagine a bent line, but this is not 3 points in some 2D space - think three temperatures or three light levels. They are necessarily linear because they measure a single property.

There are two options here. 

If the second segment attaches to the end point of the first, the length increases. This is addition, even if we don't have a number for it. If that second object happens to point the other way that is subtraction.

If the two start points attach together, they are parallel can can be compared. This forms a ratio, and is the basis for multiplication and division.
