# Overview of Concept initial structures

These structures are used to conceptualize some field or space, in a very general sense.
Values/equations have three abilities - 1) to describe or create state, 2) to describe or enact change, and 3) to describe, predict or enact recipies/equations/patterns.

## StatePrimitives
To conceptualize a space, you start with primitive patterns. While the choice of these matter, they can be teased out mathematically by reducing correlation.
Primitive gradients only reference themselves, and absence of themselves. So red to black, voltage to ground, height to floor, volume to quiet etc.
These are considered primitives, because they describe the field as it exists, and the default (with one sample) is it will continue to exist unchanged.
In electronic circuits the base primitives are current, voltage, and frequency. This is the input of the circuit.
At a higher level, you can of course control voltage with batteries, frequency with generators etc, but that would be characterizing a different space.
The state of a space can be characterized in any way, just it is assumed to be 'truth' by the mechanism probing it.

There is a common pattern of 3 primitives, which can be thought of as momentum, change, and repetition. 
This exists in electricity, mechanics, flow, sound, stories, equations, and numbers themselves.
Using these to start exploring spaces, it certainly can get more complex (eg phase of repetion can matter), but these cover a lot.
Spaces also interact with each other of course, color and heat for example, so this needs to be able to dynamically create new spaces from combining or disecting sub spaces.
### Momentum
Requires one sample of information, describes the size of the state, alluding to the scale, the the inertia, how hard it might be to change.
This is current, volume, force, the day to day of the main character before a story arc kicks in. Like how wide a pipe is.
### Change
Requires two samples of information, describes a change, or a potential change. 
This is voltage, pressure, the goal in a story etc. Like how fast water is flowing in a pipe.
### Cycles
Requires at least three samples of information, a smaple, a change and a repeat (repeat is a single sample in numbers, think exponentiation).
This is often frequency, but doesn't need to be simple. A AC signal, a fading spring, a texture, a slow beat, a complex rhythm, even the tempo of a three pattern setup joke.
### Noise
Unstated, but important, is noise. Noise is any signal that is not captured, understood, or used by primitives, but still affects the system.
Most signals are a combination of primitives and noise, and the goal of conceptualizing a space is to maximize the predictive encoding and minimize the noise.
There is only one noise class, naturally, as the only way to tell one set of noise from another is by characterizing some aspect of it (which subtracts that part, and pure noise remains).

### Gradients
These are not new measures, just ways of combining primitives, so samples from a space can be ordered in different ways that help with recording, understanding, and manipulating.
All information exists on a gradient of some sort. Gradients just mean values can be ordered in some way, and that 'way' is what defines the gradient.
The 'increasing' part of a gradient is easy as that is the name of the concept the gradient represents.
A gradient is always defined against an 'opposite', and ultimately that opposite involves a difference from an 'external' state space.
The opposite end can be different things depending on context.
For example, 12v is relative to ground, but the gradient may go to -12v, or it may be in terms of a 120v AC signal.
'Red' may fade to dark red and black, or it may fade to pink and white. Or it may increase to blue and white in terms of heat.

These gradients are manually specified to get into the flow of them, but ultimately they need to be constructed on the fly with assistance from the model/patterns of the system.
When we imagine the sound a cherry red metal rod getting dunked in a can of oil, we construct and combine gradients from our temperature/color/sound/metal/etc knowledge. 
This can be modeled as it's own space with enough experience, making the construction much faster and more accurate.
